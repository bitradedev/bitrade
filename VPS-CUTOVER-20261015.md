## AI transport comparison and user-facing Agent desks

On October 15 at 03:09–03:14 Beijing time, a read-only diagnostic compared the configured upstream and model from the production VPS and local Windows machine. Neither diagnostic submitted orders. Credentials were supplied from the existing environment and were not printed. The VPS resolved DNS in 14 ms, reached HTTPS over IPv4 in 575 ms, and fetched the authenticated model listing in 319 ms with the configured model present. Its two tiny valid JSON decisions took 5,350 ms (normal Node fetch) and 80,270 ms (forced IPv4). IPv6 had no route. From the local machine, model listing also succeeded (423 ms), but one identical decision timed out after 90 seconds and the other returned HTTP 503 in 1,321 ms. The VPS service remained active with zero unexpected restarts and about 100 MB of memory use. These observations support variable upstream response/queue availability; they do not establish a Hong Kong-only restriction or prove that a replacement server would fix it. Forced IPv4 is not a demonstrated remedy. No model, endpoint or paid service was changed.

The repair distinguishes timeout, DNS, TLS, transport, HTTP and authentication errors internally, without retaining response bodies, prompts or credentials in diagnostics. Temporary AI failures retain the last valid decision and its original timestamp, retry after the configured interval or bounded backoff (1/2/4/5 minutes), and do not invent a hold decision or trade. A successful call schedules its next evaluation from completion. Internal diagnostic timing/category samples are stored only in the server database, with 16 recent entries and UTC daily counters; they are not added to the public runtime response. External upstream performance remains a dependency.
